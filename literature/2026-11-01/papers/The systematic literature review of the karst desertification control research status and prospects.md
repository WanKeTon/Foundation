# The systematic literature review of the karst desertification control research status and prospects

Check for updates

Yu Zhang<sup>1,5</sup>, Zhaohui Zhang<sup>2,5</sup>, Anjun Lan<sup>3</sup> , Li Rong<sup>3</sup> & Zhouwei Yuan<sup>4</sup>

The karst desertification (KD) control is an integrated control project aimed at ecological restoration and improving human well-being in ecologically fragile karst areas. To explore the current research status and future directions of KD control, this study systematically reviewed and analyzed 3895 articles retrieved from the WoS, Scopus, PubMed, and CNKI databases using the systematic literature review approach. Results showed that: (1) Institutions and scholars from South China played a leading role in this research field. (2) Research focuses on theory research, technology development, model construction, application demonstration, and monitoring and evaluation. (3) The annual publication volume can be divided into initial, rapid, and stable development stages. (4) The major global advancements and landmark achievements have been systematically mapped, along with the key scientific and technological issues to be solved in the world. This study bridges KD control gaps from emerging challenges, guiding future research and strategies.

Karst is widely distributed globally, with major concentrations along the Mediterranean coast of Europe, the Eastern United States, and Southern China<sup>1–3</sup>. Karst is a term used to describe a special style of landscape containing caves and extensive underground water systems. The principal karst rocks are carbonate rocks and evaporite rocks, in the aggregate their surface and near-surface outcrops occupy \~ 20% of the planet’s dry ice-free land. Roughly 20-25% of the global population depends largely or entirely on groundwater obtained from them<sup>4</sup>. The karst region in South China covers over 55×10<sup>4</sup> km<sup>2</sup>, making it one of the most typical, complex, and diverse tropical-subtropical karst landscapes in the world. It features a distinct dual surface-subsurface structure and is the largest and most contiguous ecologically fragile karst area globally, home to approximately 10% of China’s population<sup>5–7</sup>. Karst resource environments exhibit four typical characteristics: vulnerability, sensitivity, susceptibility, and low natural regeneration capacity<sup>8</sup>. Their vulnerability is reflected in high sensitivity to ecological variation, low environmental carrying capacity, limited disaster resilience, and difficulty in recovery after damage<sup>5</sup>. KD represents the extreme manifestation ofkarst ecological degradation. KD is an evolutional process ofthe land surface that causes deforestation, soil erosion, gradual exposure of rocks, and great loss of land productivity in prominent human-land conflicts, visually resembling a desert landscape due to unreasonable human socio-economic activities in the fragile ecological environment ofthe karst<sup>9</sup>.

Once initiated, this process is challenging to reverse and significantly impacts the local population’s production and daily life<sup>10,11</sup>.

Chinese scholars were the first to propose the concepts of KD and its classification, conducting studies on its formation mechanisms and spatiotemporal evolution<sup>2,9,12,13</sup>. The Chinese government has placed great importance on KD control, incorporating it into key ecological restoration initiatives<sup>14</sup>. Kangning Xiong, Zhongcheng Jiang, and Kelin Wang, among others, led research teams to study and demonstrate KD control, developing scientific models such as “Huajiang”, “Qingzhen”, “Bijie”, “Huanjiang”, and “Pingguo”<sup>10,15,16</sup>. Through promotion and application, they proposed a universal ecological-industrial engineering model for KD control, achieving a balance between ecological restoration and human well-being<sup>17</sup>. The ecological industries derived from KD control were used in targeted poverty alleviation trials, breaking the vicious cycle of ecological vulnerability and rural poverty<sup>18,19</sup>. Countries and regions such as the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, and Slovenia also exhibit occurrences of KD. However, these areas generally have milder desertification, lower population density, and less human interference, with research focusing primarily on karst hydrology, geomorphology, and soil issues<sup>20–22</sup>. Therefore, KD control is largely unique to China.

The 5th United Nations Environment Assembly, held in 2021, focused on the theme “Strengthening nature conservation actions to achieve the sustainable development goals.” It called for global cooperation to intensify efforts in protecting and restoring natural resources and adopted naturebased conservation solutions to promote sustainable development. KD control, as an effective approach to promoting sustainable development, is crucial for improving the quality of the karst ecological environment and contributes to Earth’s health and human well-being. However, KD control still faces challenges such as increasing complexity, insufficient sustainability, and difficulty in maintaining long-term effectiveness. Currently, most reviews on KD control are either narrative literature reviews<sup>23</sup> or bibliometric analyses<sup>24</sup>. Some reviews merely summarize the status of KD control in specific regions<sup>25</sup>, lacking global scientific discussion and synthesis. Therefore, this study employs a systematic literature review approach<sup>26</sup> to systematically review the achievements in KD control, clarify the current state of research, identify key scientific issues, and highlight future research directions. This is of significant importance for advancing scientific KD control, improving the ecological environment, and enhancing human wellbeing. This study addresses the following three questions: (1) the distribution of institutions and authors, research content, hotspots and trends, and research stages of literature on KD control; (2) the major global develop ments and landmark achievements in theory research, technology development, model construction, application demonstration, and monitoring and evaluation; (3) the key scientific and technological issues to be solved in the world and future research prospects. This study adopts a multidimensional framework to synthesize global research on KD control, addressing the existing gap in comprehensive global reviews in this field. The review of the current research landscape offers practitioners and researchers a comprehensive understanding of KD control. By identifying pressing scientific questions based on the current state ofresearch, this study highlights future challenges and outlines directions for further investigation.

## Methods

## Literature retrieval and method

This study conducted a systematic literature review following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines<sup>27</sup>. These guidelines ensure methodological transparency, reproducibility, and bias minimization while enhancing the overall quality of systematic reviews. The PRISMA methodology has been widely adopted and validated across disciplines, including ecology, medicine, and psychology, demonstrating scientific rigor through peer-reviewed confirmations<sup>28–30</sup>.

## Literature search and retrieval

Literature searches were conducted in Web of Science (WOS), Scopus, PubMed, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). The search timeframe spanned from the inception of each database through December 31, 2023. Since research on KD control technologies and models is unique to China<sup>18</sup>, we have chosen these databases to make the best effort in acquiring relevant research findings on the subject. At the same time, it also includes relevant studies from major international databases, aiming to obtain research results on KD control from a global perspective. The search terms included “<sup>喀斯特石漠化</sup>/<sup>岩溶石漠化</sup>/<sup>石漠化</sup>karst desertification/ karst rocky desertification/rocky desertification + 防治/治理control/ management/rehabilitation/governing/prevention/harness,” initially yielding 5305 records.

## Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

This study established explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria to systematically identify relevant literature for critical review. Inclusion criteria: (1) Publications from database inception through December 31, 2023; (2) Focus on KD control; (3) Search terms present in title, abstract, or keywords; (4) Chinese or English language publications. Exclusion criteria: (1) Unavailable full-text documents; (2) Peripheral relevance to KD control; (3) Non-Chinese/English publications; (4) Literature with quality assessment below 6 points; (5) Repeated articles.

## Literature Search Results

The literature selection process followed the PRISMA framework, comprising three phases: identification, screening, and included (Fig. 1). The identification phase yielded 5305 records through database searches using established search strings within the specified timeframe. During screening, 422 unavailable and duplicate records were initially excluded. Title, abstract and keywords screening subsequently eliminated 706 publications with peripheral relevance to KD control, retaining 4177 entries for full-text evaluation. Full-text assessment excluded 282 articles based on rigorous relevance criteria and quality assessment, resulting in 3895 high-quality publications focused on KD control. These included 447 English-language records: 419 journal articles, 24 conference papers, 1 book, and 3 editorial materials. The 3448 Chinese-language records comprised 1592 journal articles, 316 theses or dissertations, 118 conference papers, 262 newspapers, 1 book, 788 yearbooks, 309 patents, 1 standard, and 61 achievements.

## Quality assessment

A hybrid quality appraisal checklist integrating quantitative and qualitative criteria was applied to evaluate the methodological rigor of included publications<sup>31</sup>. For each article, we assigned a score based on 10 evaluation criteria (Table 1). The criteria for classifying literature quality were as follows: articles scoring below 5 were considered low quality, while those scoring 6 or above were classified as high quality. Publications scoring below 5 points were systematically excluded to ensure methodological quality threshold compliance. During the full-text screening phase of the aforementioned literature search, we concurrently implemented a quality appraisal process. Two reviewers independently scored each article. In cases of disagreement, a third reviewer provided an additional score, and a final score was determined based on consensus. At the full-text review stage, lowquality articles accounted for 7%, while high-quality articles made up 93%. This dual-stage evaluation resulted in the final inclusion of3895 studies, all demonstrating quality scores of 6 or above (Fig. 2). This rigorous process ensured the inclusion of 3895 publications with validated methodological integrity. We acknowledge the potential bias inherent in subjective evaluations; however, the quality assessment process substantially reduced the influence oflow-quality literature on the study’s conclusions. The inclusion of high-quality literature enhanced the robustness of conclusions drawn in the review of rocky desertification control research.

## Synthesis

At this stage, we extensively reviewed 3895 articles, extracting and classifying relevant data to derive knowledge and draw conclusions. We integrated data from articles across four databases. We compiled publication counts for each institution and categorized them by type. We analyzed the number of publications published by the author, citation frequency, and collaborative authorship mapping, disambiguating author names where necessary. We also categorized the included articles by research content and manually refined the classifications to ensure their accuracy. Current research on KD control focuses on exploring theory research, technology development, model construction, application demonstration, and monitoring and evaluation<sup>9,15</sup>. This method of topic classification has been widely applied in bibliometric and synthesis studies related to rocky desertification<sup>26,32,33</sup>. We believe that focusing on these five dimensions is essential for a comprehensive understanding of KD control. For keyword distribution, we consolidated the included keywords. For the annual distribution of publications, we applied a polynomial fitting curve and calculated the inflection points to delineate the research stages. This approach to dividing research stages has been widely used in bibliometric studies<sup>34,35</sup>. More detailed information and steps regarding data processing can be found in the Supplementary Information.

## Distribution of research content

The literature on KD control can be categorized into theory research (25%), technology development (14%), model construction (20%), application demonstration (24%), and monitoring and evaluation (17%) (Fig. 6). Theory studies on KD control are primarily found in journal articles, theses, and conference papers. Technology development is predominantly reflected in patents and technical achievements, while model construction is frequently presented in journal articles, newspapers, and theses. Application demonstrations are mostly published in yearbooks, journal articles, and newspapers, and monitoring and evaluation are focused on journal articles and theses. These trends highlight the academic and professional nature of theoretical studies, the applied orientation of technological development, the academic value and societal influence of model construction, the effectiveness and progress of application demonstrations, and the standardized and scientific approach ofmonitoring and evaluation. The dominance of theoretical studies and application demonstrations in KD research indicates the need to strengthen the development and innovation of KD control technologies, the construction and promotion of models, and the improvement and refinement of monitoring and evaluation systems in future applied research.

## Distribution of hotspots and trends

High-frequency keywords indicate that karst desertification (2247), karst (492), comprehensive control (439), karst desertification control (427), karst areas (354), countermeasures (243), current situation (211), Guizhou (194), cause (166), and vegetation restoration (151) are research hotspots (Fig. 7). Soil (52), vegetation (48), and bare slopes (9) are key natural factors for identifying KD and serve as the foundation for control efforts. Studies on soil erosion (124), soil organic carbon (35), and soil nutrients (19) focus on the physicochemical properties of soil, facilitating vegetation selection for restoration. Land use (75), ecological reconstruction (23), and poverty alleviation (15) reveal that rational development and utilization ofresources while ensuring protection have become key aspects ofKD control. Soil and water conservation (74), afforestation technology (28), and control tech nology (23) reflect major control techniques, while technology integration (22) and technology systems (15) represent emerging trends.

Comprehensive control (439), models (125), and control model (16) highlight the focus on typical control models, model integration, and industrial models as current hotspots and trends. Restoration efforts have focused on forest vegetation recovery<sup>51</sup>, soil and water conservation<sup>52</sup>, and ecological agriculture<sup>53</sup> through control models and pilot demonstrations. Ecosystem services (35), ecological industry (32), and ecological effects (28) indicate that demonstration projects have yielded significant benefits for improving ecosystems and human well-being. Among these, the ecological industry emphasizes the application of new theories and technologies and the response to policies, addressing gaps in mechanisms, technology, and market support<sup>54,55</sup>. Benefit evaluation (61), GIS (42), remote sensing (37), evaluation (29), and monitoring (11) reflect advancements in monitoring technologies. Remote sensing and GIS have been utilized to monitor KD<sup>56</sup>, community dynamics<sup>57</sup>, and the spatial distribution and variation of biological features<sup>58</sup>, providing essential data for evaluating control outcomes.

The third stage, from 2008 to 2023, shows a fluctuating trend with an average of over 150 publications per year, marking stable development with increasingly refined and innovative research topics.

## Major global developments and landmark achievements

Theoretical research serves as the fundamental basis for KD control, offering mechanistic insights and guiding the direction of technology development. Technology development translates theoretical findings into practice, develops key technologies based on theory, and provides technical support for model construction. Model construction integrates theory and technology to develop replicable solutions and guides the layout of demon stration projects. Application demonstration tests the feasibility of KD control models, while the resulting benefits are subject to monitoring and evaluation. Monitoring and evaluation quantify the effectiveness of control through the construction of multidimensional indicator systems, thereby driving iterative improvements in control projects. In summary, theory research, technology development, model construction, application demonstration, and monitoring and evaluation collectively form a systematic framework that links upstream fundamental research with down stream practical assessment in KD control (Fig. 9).

## Theory research

(1) Based on the ecological characteristics ofKD, the spatial heterogeneity of vegetation growth environments was identified, and plant restoration strategies were proposed.

The soil layers in KD areas are exceptionally shallow and rich in calcium. The interlacing ofrocks and thin soil layers creates diverse and highly heterogeneous microhabitats<sup>59,60</sup>. Additionally, due to uneven precipitation distribution, karst desertification regions often face a threat of drought, making water supply in the soil even more unstable<sup>61</sup>. Therefore, vegetation restoration strategies for KD areas have been studied extensively, including research on surface and subsurface soil erosion<sup>62</sup>, soil nutrient stoichiometry<sup>63</sup>, soil succession mechanisms<sup>64</sup>, rainfall-runoff processes<sup>65</sup>, plant diversity<sup>66</sup>, climate change<sup>67</sup>, and the properties of fissured soils and water as well as plant adaptation mechanisms<sup>68</sup>. Vegetation with lithophilic, calciphilic, drought-resistant traits and well-developed root systems has been proposed for cultivation<sup>69</sup>. Proposed vegetation restoration strategies include sloped farmland conversion<sup>70</sup>, combining natural and artificial forests<sup>71</sup>, and integrating agroforestry with soil and water conservation<sup>72</sup>. For example, in nil and potential KD areas, shade-tolerant herbaceous plants are recommended, while in moderately affected areas, plants from the Rosaceae and Berberidaceae families are used for vegetation restoration<sup>73</sup>. Vegetation restoration strategies, implemented through introduction, cultivation, and protection measures, have increased vegetation coverage, achieving the goals of soil and water conservation and controling KD. Most vegetation restoration strategies involve a combination of plantations and natural forests, but their potential suppressive effects on native communities remain unclear. Therefore, future research should compare the carbon sequestration efficiency, biodiversity maintenance capacity, and disturbance tolerance thresholds of different strategies to reveal their suitability under regional hydro-soil conditions.

(2) Based on the classification and grading system of KD, different levels of restoration models have been implemented, promoting the indepth application of context-specific control theories.

The classification ofKD takes into account characteristics such as rock exposure, vegetation cover, soil cover, and slope, reflecting the differences in surface characteristics across different levels of KD<sup>9</sup>. Based on KD classification, context-specific restoration models have been implemented. On intensity and extreme intensity KD land, a mixed soil and water conservation model using Juniperus chinensis L. and Dodonaea viscosa Jacquem. has been implemented. These species are drought-resistant and adaptable to poor soils, forming a mixed-layer forest with both tree and shrub layers<sup>74</sup>. On moderate and intense KD land with certain slopes, a medicinal-type restoration model is implemented by planting Lonicera japonica Thunb., which can achieve good economic benefits in a short period<sup>75</sup>. On karst mountainous land with deep soil layers in the moderate and slight KD land, a mixed model of mountain Zenia insignis Chun and Dendrocalamus minor (McClure) L.C. Chia & H.L. has been implemented, forming mixed forests that accelerate vegetation restoration<sup>76</sup>. On potential, slight, and moderate KD land, particularly in dry, hot river valleys, an economic ecological forest model based on Zanthoxylum bungeanum Maxim. has been implemented, achieving a win-win situation for both economic and ecological benefits<sup>77</sup>. In summary, KD control strategies adopt a technical approach based on appropriate tree species combinations according to land characteristics at different levels, implementing contextspecific technical measures while balancing both ecological and economic benefits in the overall evaluation. Although technical adaptation models based on KD grading have been widely adopted in control projects, their scale dependence remains controversial. Previous classification standards based on rock exposure and slope have overlooked the regulatory roles of soil texture and fertility, water retention capacity, and soil microorganisms and fauna in vegetation restoration. Therefore, investigating vegetation restoration strategies under multi-scale coupling is a key direction for future research.

(3) Based on ecological economics theory, the concept of ecological industries for KD control has been proposed, leading to the ecological and economic development in KD areas.

In karst poverty-stricken areas, economic underdevelopment has led to exploitative practices, which have exacerbated ecosystem degradation and the spread of KD, creating a vicious cycle of “KD–poverty–KD”<sup>78,79</sup>. Therefore, in South China, through the integration of ecological economics theory and KD control, a diversified ecological economy system has been developed, including ecological circular agriculture and livestock farming, eco-tourism, bioenergy, and biopharmaceutical industries<sup>80–82</sup>. In particular, the ecological circular industries, by establishing a complete industrial chain from production—processing—energy—markets, require innovative applications of technologies in farming, livestock, processing, and energy<sup>18,83,84</sup>. This promotes close cooperation among various stakeholders, including farmers, businesses, and the government, creating a synergistic effect for improving human well-being and ecological restoration, effectively breaking the vicious cycle. In conclusion, the ecological industry model based on multi-stakeholder, multi-department, and multi-disciplinary cooperation has enhanced the compatibility of participants and improved control efficiency. However, the sustainability of the current ecological industry faces significant chal lenges, primarily because short-term economic incentives may undermine long-term ecological conservation goals. Therefore, ecological products should balance both economic and ecological benefits, with the realization of their value serving as a driving force for the development of the ecological industry.

(4) By analyzing karst ecosystem services, the theoretical and practical support for enhancing ecosystem services in KD control has been revealed.

Forest ecosystem, grassland ecosystem, wetland ecosystem, and cultivated land ecosystem in karst areas directly or indirectly provide a diverse range of services to human society, encompassing multiple functions such as food supply, energy provision, raw material production, water conservation, soil and water retention, windbreak and sand fixation, climate regulation, and greenhouse gas emission reduction<sup>85–87</sup>. Currently, research on KD focuses primarily on improving ecosystem services<sup>88,89</sup>. However, the fragility of KD ecosystems and disturbances caused by human activities have limited the improvement of ecosystem services<sup>90</sup>. Therefore, research on scientifically managing ecosystem services has been conducted, providing theoretical support for evaluating and quantifying ecosystem service values, resolving service conflicts, and optimizing resource use and management<sup>33,91,92</sup>. In practice, optimizing forest spatial structure and enhancing biodiversity have become key approaches to improving ecosystem services<sup>93–95</sup>. Enhancing ecosystem services, derivative industries of ecological control, and ecological products has become the focus of KD control<sup>96</sup>. However, current research primarily focuses on enhancing individual ecosystem services, with insufficient exploration of mechanisms for multi-service synergies. Therefore, it is necessary to develop ecosystem service flow models to identify key trade-off nodes, as well as to construct models for optimizing ecosystem functions and define technological pathways to enhance the supply capacity of ecological products.

## Technology development

(1) By implementing agricultural, biotechnology, and engineering technologies, the patterns of KD characterized by exposed rocks, improper cultivation, and soil erosion have been altered.

Agricultural technologies include farming techniques, breeding tech niques, water-saving techniques, soil cultivation techniques, intercropping and rotation techniques. For example, grass planting techniques, which involve selecting appropriate grass species, ground treatment, seeding, and subsequent management, have not only slowed down KD but also provided forage for grazing livestock, changing the traditional patterns of exposed rocks caused by cultivation<sup>97,98</sup>. Ecological restoration technologies include vegetation restoration techniques, returning farmland to forests techniques, mountain closure for afforestation techniques, land restoration techniques, and biodiversity conservation techniques. For example, the return of farmland to forest techniques has not only increased vegetation coverage but also affected the supply-demand balance of agricultural water and soil resources in the reclaimed areas, promoting resource-saving agriculture and altering unsustainable farming practices<sup>25,99</sup>. Engineering technologies include water conservancy and soil protection techniques, energy techniques, terracing techniques, slope protection techniques, and sprinkler and drip irrigation techniques (Fig. 10). For example, slope-to-terrace techniques prevent soil erosion on slopes and increase arable land by using topographic surveys, terrace design, and water source supply<sup>100</sup>. In summary, KD control technologies effectively reduce KD, enhance land resource availability, and promote the sustainable development of agricultural production. However, the application of single technologies faces challenges in long-term maintenance and lacks sustainability. Therefore, targeted coupling and integration of control technologies have become a development trend in comprehensive KD control.

(2) By implementing cultivation technology systems, ecological livestock technology systems, and ecological industrial technology systems, the synergistic operational efficiency of various technologies has been optimized.

The KD control technology system integrates individual technologies into a comprehensive system. For example, the ryegrass cultivation technology system integrates multiple technological processes, including land preparation, breeding, sowing, fertilization, irrigation, weeding, pest and disease control, and harvesting<sup>101</sup>. KD mountain mango cultivation technology system integrates techniques such as variety selection, transplanting and planting, water and fertilizer management, tree management, fruit bagging, and pest and disease control<sup>102</sup>. The ecological recycling technology system deeply integrates management technologies with the concept of ecological recycling, encompassing processes from soil enrichment and planting to biogas development and integrated farming and processing<sup>103</sup>. In summary, the technology systems demonstrate numerous advantages, including superior comprehensive effects, long-term sustainability, strong adaptability, and high efficiency. Therefore, the synergistic effects ofvarious technologies should be fully utilized, and the application and promotion of technology systems should be strongly advocated. Although these technological systems have significantly improved control efficiency through multi-link integration, there remains room for improvement in regulating carbon cycling processes in KD areas. More critically, the current systems lack proactive regulation of the coupled water-carbon processes in karst regions. Therefore, future efforts should focus on building an integrated technological system encompassing carbon capture, sequestration, trading, and energy optimization, extending the synergistic effects of these technologies to the global climate change control context.

(3) By implementing control technology integration at the county scale and the large geomorphic regional scale, a strong technological synergy has been achieved across the region.

At the county scale, typical control technology integrations include the “Six Measures for KD Control” model in Yunnan, which combines techniques such as forest closure and afforestation, terrace conversion, walnut economic forest cultivation, and ecological migration; the “Nongla” model, which integrates forest closure and afforestation with the cultivation of premium medicinal herbs; and the “Gongcheng” model, which combines bio-hedges, pioneer community arrangements, and intercropping of grain and cash crops<sup>104</sup>. This county-scale governance technology integration emphasizes specificity and effectiveness by selecting, integrating, and opti mizing appropriate technologies based on regional geography, climate, and ecological characteristics. At the large geomorphic regional scale, a comprehensive chain of technology integration has been developed, encompassing technology research and development, industry construction, and benefit evaluation. Control technologies and demonstrations for plateau mountains, basins, and gorges have been linked with industrial technologies and demonstrations for biopharmaceuticals, bioenergy, and mountain tourism, creating an ecological industry model and technology integration demonstration for karst plateau KD control<sup>18</sup>. Through the integration of mechanism analysis, technology development, and industrial demonstration, a control model and technology integration system for karst subsidence basins has been established<sup>105</sup>. The control technology integration at the large geomorphic regional scale addresses the global and regional characteristics of KD by organically integrating localized control technology results, creating a strong regional technological synergy. However, the generalizability of technology integration at the county scale is often questioned, while large geomorphic regional integration tends to overlook the heterogeneity of small watersheds. Therefore, it is necessary to develop a technical integration database for KD control, enabling region-specific allocation of technologies to address the lack of general applicability in current technology dissemination.

## Model construction

The comprehensive control and ecological agriculture-oriented model involves planting conservation forests, economic forests, and fruit-bearing forests in the upper, middle, and lower reaches of watersheds, with optimized water engineering to implement a three-dimensional ecological economic system in mountainous areas<sup>51</sup>. The karst grassland construction and ecological animal husbandry-oriented model develops herbivorous animal husbandry based on the principle of grass-livestock balance<sup>81</sup>. The vegetation restoration and ecological reconstruction model targets small watersheds by planting market-oriented economic forests, constructing supporting water facilities, and moderately developing herbivorous animal husbandry<sup>10</sup> (Fig. 12). The karst soil and water conservation and basic farmland construction-oriented model involves terrace conversion, supporting water projects, and exploring a “terrace + technology + water conservancy + industry” pathway to prosperity<sup>106</sup> (Fig. 11). The karst water resource development and irrigated agriculture-oriented model collects rainwater, develops karst water irrigation, and implements water-saving irrigation agriculture<sup>107</sup>. The karst resource utilization and eco-tourismoriented model integrates ecological control with the cultivation of ornamental trees, the development of tourism, and industrial restructuring to create a virtuous cycle of ecological and industrial development<sup>108</sup>. In summary, KD control models are based on factors such as KD grade, ecological carrying capacity, and resource endowments. They consider land use, ecological improvement, and market demand, following a development pathway that includes prerequisites—theoretical foundations—technical approaches—model promotion. The existing models face challenges in maintaining ecological-economic balance, as the overexploitation of resources has led to the degradation of native ecosystems. In addition, high control costs and difficulties in sustaining outcomes remain significant issues. Therefore, it is essential to develop a more scientific, efficient, and sustainable model for ecological industry control.

(2) A comprehensive ecological–industrial control model was constructed, forming a regional control model integration strategy of evolution mechanism—technology integration—industry coupling—control demonstration—benefit evaluation.

The synergy between KD control and ecological industries operates in two ways: on one hand, implementing KD control measures provides highquality resource conditions for ecological industry development<sup>109</sup>; on the other hand, the development of ecological industries supplies essential technical support and stable funding for KD control, promoting further control<sup>34,110</sup>. During its construction, the ecological industry control model developed a regional control integration strategy comprising evolution mechanisms—technological integration—industry coupling— contro demonstration - benefit evaluation<sup>54,111</sup>. For example, based on theories such as the karst ecological degradation mechanism, vegetation adaptation mechanism, and soil erosion mechanism, key technologies such as soil erosion control, vegetation optimization configuration, and cultivation of characteristic ecological derivative industries are integrated to implement integrated desertification control models, ecological derivative industry cultivation, and large-scale demonstration<sup>10,18</sup>. In summary, the ecological industry control model not only significantly enhances ecosystem stability but also provides abundant ecological products and services, driving the optimization and upgrading of industrial structures. However, current industrial models tend to rely excessively on a single type of ecological product. Therefore, it is necessary to apply complex adaptive systems theory to design diversified industrial networks and to develop an integrated and intelligent control model.

## Monitoring and evaluation

(1) Through an analysis of monitoring methods and indicators for KD control, it has been found that monitoring provides necessary data support for assessing the effectiveness of control measures.

There are various methods for monitoring KD, but remote sensing remains the mainstream approach for extracting information. For example, there are studies using linear spectral unmixing methods to retrieve the abundance of vegetation and exposed rocks from hyperspectral super primary ion images<sup>121</sup>. Due to the climatic conditions of karst regions and the low resolution of sensors, integrating satellite and UAV imagery for quantitative information extraction has become a popular monitoring method<sup>122</sup>. This monitoring method conducts regular and systematic observations of managed areas, collecting multidimensional data on vegetation recovery, soil erosion, and hydrological changes, thereby improving the quality of monitoring results. The indicator system for monitoring KD primarily focuses on soil erosion<sup>123</sup> , vegetation coverage<sup>124</sup> and rock exposure<sup>125</sup>. To address the complex interplay between poverty and ecological degradation, the capacity of the karst ecosystem to provide goods and services to society has also been included in the monitoring indicators<sup>126</sup>. In summary, real-time monitoring of indicators and timely adjustments to strategies and methods can prevent resource waste and delays. Although remote sensing offers wide coverage, its limited resolution leads to the missed detection of small-scale changes. Ground monitoring data are accurate but suffer from insufficient sampling density due to the complex karst terrain. Therefore, it is necessary to develop multi-source data fusion algorithms to integrate various monitoring data with ground measurements.

(2) Analysis of the benefit evaluation for KD control has revealed that the evaluation system provides a measurement standard for adjusting control methods and pathways.

The benefit evaluation system for KD control is relatively well developed in assessing both ecological and human well-being impacts. Ecological benefit evaluations focus on vegetation restoration<sup>127</sup>, soil and water conservation<sup>128</sup>, soil quality<sup>129</sup>, carbon sequestration<sup>130</sup>, water resource carrying capacity<sup>131</sup>, and afforestation benefits<sup>132</sup>. Human well-being evaluations focus on agricultural and pastoral production<sup>133</sup>, social welfare<sup>134</sup>, household income<sup>135</sup>, and relocation site selection<sup>136</sup>. In particular, the evaluation of ecosystem services, ecological industries, and ecological pro ducts in key areas of KD control has become a research hotspot<sup>137</sup>. Addi tionally, evaluation methods include radial basis function networks<sup>138</sup> and ecosystem health diagnostic indicator systems<sup>139</sup>. These evaluation indicators and methods, supported by benefit monitoring, accurately assess the actual impacts and effectiveness of control efforts. They identify existing problems and shortcomings in control, providing specific and practical recommendations for subsequent improvements. However, current evaluation methods lack a comprehensive framework that integrates precontrol environmental assessments, real-time monitoring and tracking during control, and comprehensive evaluations of post-control effects. Current evaluations focus on ecological and economic dimensions but overlook social and cultural aspects. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation system incorporating emerging dimensions such as cultural resilience and intergenerational equity warrants further research.

Key scientific and technological issues to be solved in the world (1) To address the scientific issue of vegetation restoration mechanisms in the binary three-dimensional structure ofkarst areas, it is necessary to clarify the coupling mechanisms between surface and subsurface water–soil processes at multiple dimensions and scales, and to develop vegetation restoration strategies that combine water conservation, resource optimization, and soil protection.

Current research primarily focuses on the dynamic relationships between surface soil erosion, elevation, soil organic matter, soil thickness, planting density, vegetation types, groundwater utilization, and vegetation growth<sup>140–143</sup>. However, vegetation restoration lacks research on the multidimensional and multiscale coupling of surface and subsurface water–soil processes. Therefore, future research on KD should focus on exploring the interactions between surface and subsurface water–soil processes during vegetation restoration, and implement strategies that combine water conservation, resource optimization, and soil protection. In terms of water conservation, it is essential to identify additional water sources and select plants with low water requirements, drought resistance, high calcium tolerance, and adaptability to poor soils. For soil protection, plants should be selected that can improve the soil conditions in rocky environments.

(2) To address the scientific issue ofoptimizing ecosystem functions in KD control, it is necessary to clarify the mechanisms of structure–function and service enhancement, reveal the trade-offs/synergies in ecosystem services, and develop models for ecosystem function optimization and technical pathways for enhancing ecological product supply capacity.

The restoration and enhancement ofecosystem functions in KD areas lie in a deep understanding of their structural composition, followed by scientific optimization to maximize service functions. Previous studies have developed ecosystem cascading frameworks, constructed ecosystem prediction models, and integrated ecosystem services with agroforestry control models to improve ecosystem structural stability, enhance service functions, and promote regional synergy<sup>32,144–146</sup>. However, current research still faces limitations in spatial scale concerning ecosystem service trade-offs/synergies, unclear driving mechanisms, and uncertainties in research outcomes<sup>33</sup>. Therefore, future research on KD control should adopt multi-scale dynamic research methods to quantify the relationship between ecosystem structure and function, and reveal the mechanisms ofservice trade-offs/synergies and function optimization. Additionally, models should be developed to systematically optimize ecosystem functions, focusing on supply–demand dynamics, stakeholders, and market transactions. Moreover, technical pathways should be proposed to enhance the supply capacity of ecological products, addressing the bottlenecks in function-driven enhancement of ecological product supply capacity<sup>147</sup>.

(3) To address the frontier scientific issue ofhow to realize the value of ecological products and drive the formation of ecological industries in KD control, it is necessary to clarify the mechanisms ofecological product value production, demand, and supply, reveal the chain mechanism of ecological product value realization—ecological industry formation—rural ecological industry revitalization, and develop models and pathways for rural ecological industry revitalization and ecological product value realization.

The ecological products generated by the karst desertification control such as water, soil, grass, forest and fruits, have important economic and social values, which can provide diversified sources of income and employment opportunities for villages, form ecological industries with regional characteristics and competitive advantages<sup>55,148</sup>. By integrating ecological products such as specialized and efficient forests, grassland livestock farming, agroforestry, and mountain tourism in KD control, a scaled and specialized composite system for the ecological industry in KD can be developed to drive the growth of the ecological industry<sup>149</sup>. However, these studies on ecological product value realization primarily focus on the supply side, with insufficient attention given to the demand side. Furthermore, most studies focus on enhancing the realization of ecological product value to drive the development of ecological industries, but there is a lack of research linking this with improvements in social well-being and ecological benefits<sup>150</sup>. Therefore, future research on KD control should clarify the mechanisms of ecological product value production, demand, and supply on the demand side, reveal the chain mechanism ofecological product value realization—ecological industry formation—rural ecological industry revitalization, and establish a model for rural ecological industry revitalization and ecological product value realization that incorporates scientific evaluation—market-based operations—ecological industry development.

(4) To address the frontier scientific issue of enhancing carbon sequestration capacity in KD areas, an integrated technological system for carbon capture, carbon storage, carbon trading, and energy optimization needs to be developed.

The unique geological structure and hydrological conditions of karst regions result in significant differences in carbon cycling processes compared to non-karst areas. The carbon sequestration capacity ofkarst regions is primarily influenced by factors such as KD, water scarcity, and human activities, which introduce significant uncertainty and potential<sup>137,151</sup>. Studies have found that the karst water-carbon coupling cycle can promote vegetation growth, enhancing carbon cycling and carbon sequestration in karst areas<sup>152</sup>. However, there are deficiencies in the management and utilization of water and carbon resources in KD areas. Therefore, future KD control should develop an integrated technological system encompassing carbon capture, carbon storage, carbon trading, and energy optimization to enhance carbon sequestration capacity.

(5) To address the frontier scientific issue of the lack of optimized configuration in KD control technologies, an integrated technology library system for KD control needs to be established.

Current KD control technologies primarily focus on water and soil conservation through water-saving and soil-enrichment techniques<sup>153</sup>. Some studies propose integrating techniques for afforestation, economic forest and grassland planting, and specialized economic fruit tree cultivation, tailored for different levels of KD<sup>154</sup>. However, this approach to technology integration only considers the level ofKD, lacking a comprehensive analysis of natural and cultural contexts, as well as governance needs. Therefore, future KD control must integrate and innovate biological, engineering, and agricultural technologies, establishing an integrated technology library system to rationally combine and configure control technologies.

(6) To address the frontier scientific issue of the urgent need for transforming KD control models, it is essential to improve the ecological product market system and the ecological compensation system, and to build an ecological industry control model.

The unique KD control models in South China, such as the model of “Kenfu”, “Huajiang”, “Guohua”, and “Bijie”, have achieved significant results in improving regional ecological environments and supporting poverty alleviation efforts<sup>111</sup>. However, in KD areas, there are still issues such as low farmer participation in control, insufficient introduction of market mechanisms, high management costs, and the difficulty of maintaining results. Therefore, future KD control should improve the ecological product market system and the ecological compensation system, and promote the integration and collaboration ofecological industries through technological innovation, information sharing, and resource sharing, to build a more scientific, efficient, and sustainable ecological industry control model.

(7) To address the frontier scientific issue ofthe lack ofsustainability in KD control models, it is essential to integrate ecological elements such as mountains, water, forests, fields, lakes, grasslands, and wetlands to develop an integrated intelligent control model.

KD control projects have achieved significant results in areas such as the Karst Plateau, peak-cluster depressions, fault basins, and valley regions<sup>18,54,105,155</sup>. However, due to the complexity of the natural and social environment in KD areas, the control models tend to focus on short-term results, lacking long-term sustainability. This has led to limited scalability of control models, slow ecological recovery rates, and unstable economic benefits. Currently, research on the integration of control technologies and models mostly remains at the theoretical discussion stage, with a lack of practical application studies<sup>156</sup>. Therefore, future KD control should integrate ecological elements such as mountains, water, forests, fields, lakes, grasslands, and wetlands, and incorporate key common technologies. Additionally, modern information technologies such as big data and cloud platforms should be utilized to develop comprehensive control solutions and establish an integrated intelligent control model.

(8) To address the increasing difficulty of KD control, it is essential to reveal the socio-ecological system’s synergies in these regions and develop an integrated control demonstration model combining theory, technology, and application.

The socio-ecological systems in KD areas are fragile, and coupled with unsustainable human activities, the region has undergone severe environmental degradation <sup>157,158</sup>. Whether the conflict between the ecological environment and local human well-being can be effectively resolved is key to the sustainable development ofthe region<sup>159,160</sup>. However, the understanding of the structure and optimization of the socio-ecological system, as well as the trade-offs and synergies ofits services, remains insufficient in KD areas. Furthermore, this lack ofunderstanding limits the assessment ofthe value of socio-ecological system services in KD areas and hinders the development of control models aimed at further enhancing human well-being, making it difficult to overcome the constraints on improving the quality and efficiency of control projects. Therefore, in-depth research on the complexity and dynamics of the socio-ecological system’s synergies in KD areas is needed. This should be coupled with deeper studies on regional prevention and control, collaboration, and the development of an integrated control demonstration model combining theory, technology, and application.

(9) To address the frontier scientific issue of the difficulty in consolidating poverty alleviation results in KD areas, it is necessary to develop regional industry clusters from the exploitation of ecological resources, ecological industry chains, and ecological products.

KD exhibits significant spatial aggregation, and the degree ofKD often shows an antagonistic coupling relationship with poverty incidence<sup>161</sup>. This is because poor counties align geographically with karst regions and areas severely affected by KD<sup>162</sup>. Typical poverty alleviation relocation is considered an effective strategy to address spatial poverty traps<sup>163,164</sup>. However, issues such as the subsequent industrial development and employment after relocation in KD areas leave relocated households still facing the risk of falling back into poverty, making it difficult to consolidate poverty alleviation results. Therefore, future KD control must effectively connect poverty alleviation with rural revitalization, exploit ecological resources in relocation areas, improve ecological industry chains, develop distinctive ecological products, realize the value ofecological products, and form regional industry clusters, which are key to achieving rural revitalization in KD areas.

(10) To address the frontier scientific issue of an incomplete monitoring network for KD control, it is necessary to integrate multi-source monitoring data with ground-based measurements and develop emerging technologies for extracting key information from images.

In KD monitoring, techniques such as spectral decomposition, stratified environmental reference, and spectral unmixing are used to extract key information about KD<sup>121,165,166</sup>. However, these monitoring methods for KD are limited by slow data update speeds, low resolution, and susceptibility to interference from weather factors like clouds and rain. More importantly, the lack of ground-based measurement data undermines the accuracy and reliability of the monitoring results. Therefore, future KD control must combine multi-source monitoring data with ground-based measurements and use emerging technologies to automatically extract and identify key information from images, thereby creating a more accurate and reliable monitoring network.

## Discussion

This review is based on the retrieval and screening of 3895 articles from WoS, Scopus, PubMed, and CNKI: (1) The number of publications and citation frequency indicate that institutions and authors from South China dominate the field. Future research could expand to include northern China and promote international collaboration in KD control. (2) The literature is categorized into theory research, technology development, model construction, application demonstration, and monitoring and evaluation. (3) Keywords such as karst desertification, karst, comprehensive control, karst desertification control, karst areas, countermeasures, current situation, Guizhou, cause, and vegetation restoration reveal the hotspots and trends in KD research. (4) The research is divided into three stages: initial development, rapid development, and stable development.

Major global developments and landmark achievements have pro vided valuable insights for KD control. Through investigations of the karst natural environment, the spatial heterogeneity of vegetation growth conditions was revealed, leading to the development of plant restoration strategies. The integration of ecological economics theory and KD control has facilitated the development and construction ofecological industries for KD control. The classification and grading ofKD follows the theory oflocalized adaptation. Research on karst ecosystem services has revealed both theoretical and practical insights into enhancing ecosystem services. Agricultural, biological, and engineering technologies have transformed the patterns of rocky exposure, improper farming, and soil erosion in KD. Systems such as cultivation techniques, ecological livestock farming, and ecological industry technologies have optimized the synergistic benefits of various technologies. The integration ofcontrol technologies at both count and large-scale geomorphic levels has created a powerful technological synergy across the region. The KD control and ecological industry models have achieved a positive interaction between ecological protection and economic development. The construction and promotion of demonstration projects have led to significant benefits in both ecological and human welfare. The establishment ofmonitoring methods and indicators has provided essential data support for evaluating control effectiveness. The establishment ofa control benefit evaluation system has driven the optimization and improvement of control strategies.

Key scientific and technological issues to be solved in the world highlight the future directions ofresearch and development. Clarify the coupling mechanisms between multi-dimensional, multi-scale surface and subsurface water-soil processes and vegetation restoration, and develop adaptive plant restoration strategies for water conservation and soil ecological restoration. Explain the mechanisms of structurefunction and service enhancement, uncover the trade-offs/synergies and optimization mechanisms of services, and construct models for ecosystem function optimization and pathways for improving ecological product supply capacity. Clarify the production-demand-supply mechanisms of ecological product value, reveal the chain mechanism of ecological product value realization, ecological industry formation, and rural ecological industry revitalization, and construct models for rural ecological industry revitalization and pathways for realizing ecological product value. Develop an integrated technological system for carbon capture, carbon sequestration, carbon trading, and energy optimization. Build an integrated technology library system for KD control. Improve the ecological product market system and ecological compensation system, and establish ecological industry control models. Coordinate ecological elements such as mountains, waters, forests, farmland, lakes, and grasslands to develop an integrated intelligent control model. Uncover the socio-ecosystem synergy mechanisms in KD areas and create integrated theoretical, technical, and applied control models. Develop local industry clusters based on the development of ecological resources, ecological industry chains, and ecological products. Integrate multi-source monitoring data with groundtruth data and leverage emerging technologies to develop image key information recognition techniques.

There are some limitations to this article. Firstly, we only searched the WoS Core Collection, Scopus, PubMed, and CNKI databases, without including relevant literature from other databases. This may lead to an incomplete and one-sided study. Secondly, we made every effort to use various synonyms of “the karst desertification” and “control” as search terms to help us obtain more literature. However, due to the complexity and limitations ofthe literature database search engines and methods, our search for relevant literature is still incomplete. Thirdly, although we attempted to maintain objectivity when summarizing and analyzing literature, statistics and visualization mappings, this process is still subjective. Finally, while we described the models, technologies, and demonstration projects related to KD control, the lack of comparative analysis limited our ability to evaluate their constraints and regional applicability.

Karst regions worldwide commonly face challenges ofKD, yet research outside China remains significantly limited. Existing international literature indicates that most countries still lack comprehensive studies on the driving mechanisms ofKD and the development ofsystematic control strategies. To address this transboundary ecological challenge, strengthening international scientific collaboration can accelerate the exchange and iterative improvement of control technologies and restoration models, while also providing practical references for countries and regions in urgent need of KD control. Notably, secondary disasters, poverty traps, and ecological security risks resulting from KD have become systemic barriers to sustainable development in many countries. To build a scientifically grounded control framework, it is essential to transcend traditional disciplinary boundaries and integrate theories and methodologies from the humanities and social sciences, climatology, ecology, and other fields, thereby estab lishing a “geology–ecology–society” interdisciplinary research system to support the formulation of comprehensive and feasible control strategies.
