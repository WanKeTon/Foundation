## A B S T R A C T

Although previous studies have shown that bedrock composition regulates vegetation drought vulnerability, the variability in the response and recovery mechanisms of vegetation under different lithologic contexts remain unclear. Based on the record-breaking extreme drought that occurred in Southwest China in 2022, this study integrated multi-source remote sensing data to systematically assess productivity loss, vegetation recovery time, and their main controlling factors across karst regions (dolomite and limestone) and non-karst regions (clastic rocks). The results showed that vegetation in dolomite areas experienced the most severe productivity loss, maximum GPP (Gross Primary Productivity) loss of shrubland reached 494 kg C/m², 2.57 times higher than that in clastic rock regions, and the average recovery time after drought reached 5.08 months, 1.60 times longer than in clastic regions. Importance analysis indicated that WUE (Water Use Efficiency) was the key factor affecting vegetation recovery in dolomite and limestone regions, with importance values of 0.76 and 0.35, respectively; whereas, temperature was the dominant factor in clastic rocks region (importance value = 0.85). Although vegetation in limestone areas recovered slightly faster than in dolomite, it was still limited by weak WUE. Coupled analysis of WUE and SSM (Surface Soil Moisture) revealed that WUE decreases with greater water availability in limestone and dolomite areas but increases in clastic regions, reflecting a higher water-use responsiveness of vegetation on clastic bedrock. This indicates that the limited soil water-holding capacity in karst regions restricts the potential for efficient water use under high-moisture conditions.

## 1. Introduction

Under the background of global change, the increasing frequency of extreme drought events has significantly exacerbated the uncertainty of forest ecosystem recovery and shows a continuously intensifying trend (Chen et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2024b). Studies have shown that from 2001 to 2022, vegetation resistance to sudden droughts declined by approximately 27 % in regions such as East Asia, western North America, and northern Europe (Zhang et al., 2025). In the context of large-scale ecological restoration, the karst region of Southwest China has become an important contributor to global greening in recent years (Tong et al., 2020). However, the special geological structure and limited soil water-holding capacity have made this region more prone to frequent temporary droughts (Jiang et al., 2020; Yue et al., 2023). For example, the severe drought in 2009–2010 offset years of carbon sink recovery, leading to a temporary drop in the carbon sink/emission ratio to zero (Tong et al., 2020). During the record-breaking summer-autumn drought in 2022, the threshold effect of surface soil moisture triggered a sharp decline in forest productivity (Zhao et al., 2023). Recent studies have shown that bedrock composition also plays a critical role in controlling the drought vulnerability of vegetation (Callahan et al., 2022). These observations emphasize the urgent need to clarify the drought response mechanisms of fragile ecosystems in the karst region of Southwest China.

Most existing studies have focused on the direct impacts of extreme droughts on vegetation growth. Recent drought events have led to nearly twice the global average gross primary productivity (GPP) loss compared with previous periods (Xu et al., 2019), and a drought in 2023 demonstrated a record decline in GPP under high soil aridity in southwest China (Wang et al., 2025b). However, vegetation recovery capacity is considered more crucial than drought resistance for maintaining ecosystem function (Xiao et al., 2024b), as the rate of recovery not only determines short-term functional restoration but also reflects the long-term stability and resilience of ecosystems (Smith et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025a). Ecosystems with slow recovery are more likely to experience persistent degradation under future extreme climatic conditions (Luo et al., 2015). In this context, the ability of vegetation to both resist productivity loss and recover rapidly and effectively is emerging as a key indicator of ecological adaptability and sustainability (Yao et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2021a). Furthermore, compared with other regions, the unique water utilization strategies in Southwest China’s karst areas make the vegetation response mechanism to drought more complex (Ding et al., 2021b; Li et al., 2021; Song et al., 2024). In addition to the greater physiological stress caused by shallow and discontinuous soils during drought (Xu et al., 2021), lithological heterogeneity also shapes spatial differences in vegetation recovery (He et al., 2022). Therefore, identifying the key drivers of post-drought vegetation recovery and elucidating their spatial variability in karst regions is essential for enhancing ecosystem resilience.

Vegetation productivity loss and recovery time, as important indicators of ecosystem drought resilience, are co-regulated by multiple ecological and environmental factors, including soil moisture, water use efficiency (WUE), and vegetation structure (Anderegg et al., 2018; Li et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2025; Schwalm et al., 2017). Especially in the karst regions of Southwest China, studies have shown that vegetation growth is highly dependent on water use efficiency (WUE) due to the well-developed karst fissures and the uneven spatial distribution of soil water (Liu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2024a)Liu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2024a. Of particular importance is that the mechanisms by which these drivers influence recovery may differ significantly under contrasting geological conditions (Wang et al., 2024b). Previous studies have shown that in regions affected by severe rocky desertification, lithological constraints substantially influence vegetation mortality and recovery potential (Xiao et al., 2024a). However, systematic comparisons on the processes of vegetation productivity loss and recovery in karst regions across different lithologic types $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . } } \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g . } } _ { }$ , dolomite, limestone, and clastic rocks) are still relatively limited, especially in terms of spatial hetero geneity and its underlying mechanisms (Shi et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2022). This raises several key questions: Are there significant differences in vegetation productivity loss and recovery time under different lithological conditions after extreme drought? What are the main ecological and environmental drivers affecting vegetation productivity loss and recovery?

To address these scientific questions, this study focuses on the record-breaking summer–autumn drought event in Southwest China in 2022. Using multi-source remote sensing data, we systematically assessed the vegetation productivity loss and recovery time and its main controlling factors in karst (dolomite, limestone) and non-karst (clastic) regions, with particular emphasis on the role of geological background. By integrating key biophysical variables - including gross primary productivity (GPP), water use efficiency (WUE), precipitation (PRE), temperature (TEM), soil thickness, and the standardized precipitation evapotranspiration index (SPEI) - and applying locally weighted regression (LOWESS), we examined the marginal effects and nonlinear contributions of different variables on recovery time. This study contributes to a better understanding of the coupled vegetation resiliencelithological features in karst ecosystems and provides scientific guid ance for ecological restoration and adaptive management in droughtprone fragile regions.

## 2. Methods and data

## 2.1. Study area

The study area is located in Southwest China, representing the primary distribution region of karst landforms in the country, including eight provinces: Guangxi, Yunnan, Guizhou, Guangdong, Hunan, Hubei, Sichuan, and Chongqing. The region is influenced by both the Southwest

Monsoon and the East Asian Monsoon, featuring distinct wet and dry seasons, with generally favorable hydrothermal conditions. Average annual precipitation is approximately 300 to 2600 mm and temperature is −19 to $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ However, constrained by its geological background, shallow soils, and poor water retention capacity, the region frequently experiences short-term drought events (Jiang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2021). Analysis of July-November SPEI (1980–2023) shows a record-breaking drought in 2022 (Fig. 1c). During this event, the relative drought index (R-SPEI) indicated anomalously dry conditions across all lithological regions (Fig. 1b). These deficits significantly constrained subsequent vegetation recovery (Fig. 1d), highlighting the strong sensitivity of vegetation dynamics to hydrothermal variability. The frequent occurrence of droughts, coupled with the complex geological background, makes this region a typical area for studying the coupled processes of moisture availability, geological conditions, and vegetation recovery.

## 2.2. Data sources

This study utilized multi-source remote sensing data and meteoro logical reanalysis products to characterize drought processes and vegetation recovery features. Gross primary productivity (GPP), as a direct measure of vegetation carbon fixation and ecosystem functioning, has been shown in several studies to be sensitive to the effects of drought (Wang et al., 2025b; Xu et al., 2019). Therefore, in this study, GPP was used to characterize vegetation before and after the drought, and the GPP data were obtained from the MODIS MOD17A2H product with a temporal resolution of 8 days (Running et al., 2015).

The drought indicator was characterized using the Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) derived from the SPEIbase v2.11 of CRU TS 4.09 dataset, which provides monthly data and is widely recognized as a reliable indicator for assessing drought severity across diverse regions (Kumar et al., 2016; Vicente-Serrano et al., 2010). The mean SPEI from July to November during 1980–2023 was used as the baseline to calculate the spatial distribution of relative drought index (R-SPEI) during the 2022 extreme drought (July-November). The calculation is defined as:

$$
R - \mathrm{SPEI} _ {2 0 2 2 (x, y)} = \frac {\mathrm{SPEI} _ {2 0 2 2 (x , y)} - \bar {S P E I} _ {m (x , y)}}{\sigma_ {S P E I , m (x , y)}}
$$

Where $S P E I _ { 2 0 2 2 ( x , y ) }$ represents the mean SPEI for July-November 2022, $S \overline { { P } } E I _ { m ( x , y ) }$ represents the multi-year mean SPEI for July-November during 1980–2023, and $\sigma _ { S P E I , m ( x , y ) }$ represents the corresponding standard deviation.

Additionally, surface soil moisture (SSM) data was obtained from Earth System Science Data (Song et al., 2022), which is based on mi crowave remote sensing and has been improved for humid regions, with a daily temporal resolution. Evapotranspiration (ET) data was sourced from the MODIS MOD16A2 product, with a temporal resolution of 8 days (Running et al., 2017). Meteorological variables were expanded to include precipitation (PRE) and temperature (TEM), both obtained from the National Tibetan Plateau Data Center, with a monthly temporal resolution (Peng et al., 2017).

The 1:500,000 lithology map and the degree of karst rocky desertification was derived from the Karst Ecosystem Science Database. Regolith thickness and soil thickness data were sourced from the Global Soil and Regolith Thickness dataset published by ORNL DAAC (Pelletier et al., 2016). The above data were synthesized into monthly and annual scales as needed during the analysis. Land use data for 2022 was also used in this study to extract vegetation regions (Yang et al., 2021), and only retained forest, shrub, and grass regions for subsequent analyses. ## 2.3. Assessment of maximum GPP loss

To identify the maximum impact of extreme drought on vegetation productivity, this study uses the period from July to November 2021 as the reference year. GPP data from July to November 2022 are extracted and compared month by month with the corresponding period in 2021 to calculate the monthly GPP difference for each pixel (i.e., GPP loss). The maximum monthly loss for each pixel is then selected to generate a raster map of the maximum GPP loss during the 2022 drought relative to the reference year. This process is implemented in Python 3.8 using the rasterio and numpy libraries, ensuring consistent reading, subtraction, and maximum value synthesis across monthly remote sensing images. The spatial reference information of the original rasters is preserved for subsequent spatial alignment and visualization analysis.

## 2.4. Assessment of vegetation recovery time

Vegetation recovery time following the 2022 extreme drought was assessed using monthly GPP data. To account for seasonal variability, a seasonally adjusted multi-year smoothing approach was applied. Specifically, for each calendar month, GPP values from 2000 to 2024 (excluding 2022) were collected to create a smoothed baseline using Savitzky-Golay filtering, with a window size of 3 years (i.e., 3 data points for each month). This approach minimizes short-term fluctuations while preserving seasonal signals. The Savitzky-Golay filtering method smooths the data through polynomial regression (Luo et al., 2005), minimizing short-term fluctuations and retaining seasonal signals. The smoothed GPP value for each month is calculated using the following formula (1):

$$
G P P _ {s m o o t h} (t) = \frac {1}{2 m + 1} \sum_ {k = - m} ^ {m} a _ {k} G P P (t + k)\tag{1}
$$

where GPP(t + k) is the original GPP value at month $t + k ,$ t represents the current month, k is the offset from the current month, $a _ { \mathrm { k } }$ are the coefficients determined by least squares fitting, and m is the window size. After the drought period (July-November 2022), recovery assessment started from December 2022. For each pixel, the observed GPP values were compared with the corresponding smoothed baselines for each month. Vegetation was considered recovered only when the observed GPP exceeded the smoothed baseline for three consecutive months. The recovery time was then defined as the number of months elapsed from December 2022 to the first occurrence of such sustained recovery. In addition, pixels with persistent missing values were excluded from the analysis. To further examine whether the maximum GPP loss and mean recovery time differed significantly among various lithological and vegetation types, we conducted a non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test.

## 2.5. Analysis of drivers of vegetation recovery time

This study employed Locally Weighted Scatterplot Smoothing (LOWESS), a non-parametric regression method, to analyze the drivers of vegetation recovery time after extreme droughts in dolomite, limestone, and clastic rocks regions (Cleveland and Devlin, 1988). The explanatory variables selected included pre-drought GPP (Pre-GPP), max GPP loss (ΔGPP), after drought water use efficiency (WUE), after drought precipitation (PRE), after drought temperature (TEM), relative drought intensity (R-SPEI), rocky desertification degree (RDD), regolith thickness (RT), and soil thickness (ST), where WUE is calculated based on the ratio of GPP and ET. The pre-drought period refers to the year before the drought event (July 2021-June 2022), and the after-drought period refers to the year after the drought ended (December 2022-November 2023).

To control for the influence of other variables, a residual-based method was applied: each driver was individually smoothed using LOWESS to reduce short-term fluctuations; simultaneously, a multiple linear regression model was used to fit the effects of all other variables except the target one on recovery time, and the residuals of recovery time were computed to represent the net effect of the target variable after removing other influences. Subsequently, the relationship between the target variable and these residuals was again smoothed using LOWESS, yielding independent response curves of each driver on recovery time, revealing differences in driver responses under varying lithological conditions.

## 2.6. Statistical analysis

To assess the relative importance of various factors influencing vegetation productivity loss and recovery time, we applied a Random Forest regression model separately to dolomite, limestone, and clastic rocks regions, using the key predictor variables mentioned above. Prior to conducting importance analysis, we first examined multicollinearity among all variables based on the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), with results indicating no multicollinearity issues (Table 2). The importance of each variable was quantified using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values, which measure the contribution of each feature to the model’s predictions. Model performance was evaluated using the coef ficient of determination (R²), with results reported for each lithologic group. Variables with statistically significant importance $( p < 0 . 0 5 )$ were highlighted in the plots.

In addition, we further analyzed the relationship between soil moisture (SSM) and water use efficiency (WUE) under different lithologic conditions to better understand how water availability interacts with WUE in different geological contexts. Hexbin scatter plots were used to visualize this relationship, combined with binned mean WUE curves. The color of the hexagons represents the density of data points.

## 3. Results

3.1. Spatial distribution of vegetation recovery time after drought and lithological variations

Following the extreme drought during the summer and autumn of 2022, vegetation recovery time exhibited pronounced spatial heterogeneity across the study area (Fig. 2). Overall, the northeastern region recovered relatively rapidly, recovering to the multi-year smoothed curve of GPP in about 1–2 months, while the southwestern and central regions took longer to recover, generally requiring 3–8 months or even longer, extending to the following year’s growing season. These areas with prolonged recovery largely overlapped with regions characterized by extensive karst development.

To further understand these spatial differences, we assessed the maximum GPP loss and vegetation recovery time among the major lithology types and vegetation types in the study area after the drought (Fig. 3). The results showed that different types of vegetation in the dolomite region experienced the highest maximum GPP loss, with mean maximum GPP loss of 644 kg $\mathrm { C } / \mathrm { m } ^ { \bar { 2 } }$ , 522 kg $\mathrm { { C / m ^ { 2 } } }$ and 494 $ { \mathrm { k g } }  { \mathrm { C } } /  {  { \mathrm { m } } ^ { 2 } }$ for forest, shrub and grass, respectively. These values were 1.14, 1.07, and 1.32 times higher than those in the limestone region, and 1.54, 1.52, and 2.57 times higher than those in the clastic rocks region (Fig. 3a).

## 3.2. Marginal effects of different factors on vegetation recovery time

To further explore the drivers of vegetation recovery time afte drought, this study analyzed the marginal effects of nine key variables based on the LOWESS curves and residual control method. The results showed that, in terms of climate and soil conditions, vegetation recovery was faster in areas with more precipitation after drought (Fig. 4d), and the thicker the soil layer, the faster the vegetation recovery (Fig. 4i). Meanwhile, the pre-drought vegetation status also affects the vegetation recovery time, and the higher the pre-drought GPP, the slower the vegetation recovery in the non-karst region of the clastic rock area compared to the karst region (Fig. 4a). It is notable that the improve ment of water use efficiency can significantly shorten the vegetation recovery time after drought (Fig. 4c). However, in the dolomite region, the rate of vegetation recovery tends to level off when WUE > 1.7. This phenomenon may be related to the region’s susceptibility to water leakage, limiting the upper limit of vegetation water utilization. In addition, vegetation recovery in dolomite areas was faster than in limestone and clastic areas when the thickness of the regolith layer was >100 m (Fig. 4h). And when the degree of rocky desertification intensifies in limestone areas, post-drought vegetation recovery becomes slower (Fig. 4g). Overall, factors such as climatic conditions, vegetation water use strategy and geological background together shaped the heterogeneous characteristics of the vegetation recovery process.

## 3.3. Factors driving GPP loss and vegetation recovery time

The importance of drivers affecting maximum GPP loss was also lithological differentiated (Fig. 5). Specifically, the greatest influence on maximum GPP loss in dolomite region was R-SPEI (0.62). Temperature was the most important factor influencing maximum GPP loss in limestone region and in clastic rocks region. A key observation is that soil thickness has a higher SHAP value for predicting maximum GPP loss in limestone region and dolomite region, indicating that thinner soil thickness in karst regions significantly exacerbates productivity loss due to drought.

The importance of drivers affecting vegetation recovery time showed significant variation across lithologies (Fig. 6). Specifically, dolomite and limestone regions had the most prominent contribution of moisturerelated factors to vegetation recovery time. The highest SHAP importance value (0.76) was found for water use efficiency in the dolomite region. Limestone region showed high importance for pre-drought GPP (0.49), water use efficiency (0.41), and post-drought precipitation (0.31). In addition, clastic rocks region was highly sensitive to temperature changes during vegetation restoration, with a SHAP importance value of 0.85.

## 4. Discussion

## 4.1. The difference of drought resilience of vegetation under various bedrocks

This study shows that lithologic conditions significantly influence vegetation response and recovery characteristics during drought. In karst regions, ecosystems developed on limestone and dolomite not only experienced a greater loss of GPP during drought, but also exhibited notably longer recovery durations than those on clastic rocks in nonkarst areas (Fig. 3a, 3 b). This discrepancy is primarily attributed to the widespread development of vertical fractures, shallow and discontinuous soil layers, and poor water-holding capacity in karst terrains (Xu et al., 2021), which limit the ability of vegetation to access stable water resources during drought events (Wang et al., 2019; Yan et al., 2019). Importantly, despite both limestone and dolomite being carbonate rocks, their ecological responses to drought differ considerably (Xiao et al., 2023).

Our results show that vegetation in dolomite-dominated areas experienced the greatest productivity loss and the longest recovery time, suggesting heightened ecosystem sensitivity to drought stress. This may be related to the prevalence of shallow-rooted shrubs and grasses in these areas, which rely heavily on near-surface water. Previous studies have indicated that shallow fracture soil water serves as a major water source for plants in karst systems - accounting for nearly 50 % of plant water use - and this pattern remains consistent between normal and drought years (Cai et al., 2025; Ding et al., 2021a). This reflects a long-term ecological adaptation to shallow soil water regimes. In contrast, limestone regions are characterized by a unique "soil trench" structure that enhances soil moisture retention, offering a more sustained water supply to plant roots during droughts. This structural advantage appears to buffer the impact of drought and confers relatively greater ecological stability compared to dolomite regions (Xu et al., 2024a; Zerga, 2024).

In addition, the coupling effect of vegetation type and lithology further exaggerates the differences in drought impacts. Although forests have the highest GPP loss in drought, they are highly capable of restoring themselves and regulating water, and benefit from their deep root systems that allow rapid access to water resources after recovery from precipitation (Maurel et al., 2020). In contrast, shrub in the karst area has a slower recovery process due to its shallow root system and unstable soil surface moisture (Chen et al., 2010). Overall, lithologic context mediates ecosystem vulnerability and resilience to extreme drought events by shaping soil-water availability and rooting strategies. These findings underscore the need to account for the interplay between geological substrates and vegetation types when developing drought adaptation strategies and ecological restoration efforts, particularly in fragile karst landscapes under increasing climate variability.

## 4.2. Factors influencing differences in drought resilience of various bedrocks

Maximum GPP loss due to drought shows clear lithologic differences, and its drivers vary depending on the geologic context (Fig. 5). Dolomite regions are more sensitive to drought severity (R-SPEI) due to their thinner soils and weak water retention capacity leading to more sensitive vegetation (Huang et al., 2025). More importantly, soil thickness had a greater effect on GPP loss during drought in the dolomite and limestone regions, suggesting that thinner soil layers in karst regions further exacerbate the negative effects of drought on vegetation productivity (Liu et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2021). Thin soil layers represent more intensive fluctuations in water availability to the vegetation root system during drought, exacerbating drought stress (Zhang et al., 2021b).

Vegetation recovery after drought depends not only on whethe rainfall occurs or on soil moisture reserves, but more critically on the ecosystem’s own capacity to acquire and efficiently use water (Wang et al., 2025a). Multivariate marginal effect analysis shows that factors such as post-drought precipitation, soil thickness, and water use efficiency (WUE) all significantly influence the recovery progress (Fig. 4). Firstly, the impact of WUE on recovery time is particularly important in karst regions, with the highest explanatory power observed in dolomite regions (Fig. 6a). This phenomenon indicates that karst ecosystems convert water inputs into productivity relatively inefficiently. Even when soil moisture conditions are not the most unfavorable, rapid water runoff and insufficient water holding capacity in karst regions still limit effective water utilization, causing extended vegetation recovery times (Liu et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2023). Further analysis reveals that this reflects differences in the adaptive strategies of vegetation to hydro logical constraints under different lithological conditions (Fig. 7). In karst regions, vegetation can enhance WUE through physiological ad justments under soil moisture limited conditions, but when soil moisture is abundant, it struggles to maintain efficient water use due to the limited soil moisture holding capacity (Luo et al., 2023). In contrast, WUE in non-karst areas exhibits greater “tolerantly” to variations in soil moisture (Cai et al., 2023). The deep soil layers in these areas enables efficient water conversion under sufficient moisture conditions by steadily increasing photosynthetic rates, allowing WUE to remain at a high level even when soil moisture is relatively abundant, consequently exhibiting more effective water use performance (Bachofen et al., 2024).

Moreover, geological attributes such as regolith thickness, though not direct drivers, indirectly regulate vegetation’s water response ability by affecting root distribution range and water acquisition pathways under different lithologic backgrounds (Pedrazas et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2023). In summary, post-drought vegetation recovery is not simply limited by water supply but is jointly determined by the ecosystem’s response thresholds of water use efficiency. Specifically, increased soil moisture does not guarantee improved water use efficiency and may even lead to its decline, potentially resulting in extended vegetation recovery time. In the complex terrain of karst regions, designing differentiated ecological restoration measures that target these specific water acquisition limitations and root adaptation differences will more effectively improve vegetation recovery speed and ecosystem stability.

## 4.3. Limitations and uncertainties

Although this study systematically assessed the post-drought vegetation recovery time and its main controlling factors based on the 2022 major drought event, certain limitations still exist. First, variables such as SSM, GPP, and WUE were all derived from remotely sensed inversion data. Influenced by the resolution and modeling algorithms, the moderating effects of microtopography and small-scale vegetation structure differences on the restoration process may not be fully captured. Second, this study mainly focuses on the effects of environmental factors on restoration, and does not consider the potential interference of land use management and other factors on restoration timing. Despite these uncertainties, the results of this study provide important insights. To promote ecological restoration in karst regions, it is difficult to accelerate vegetation recovery by relying on water input, and more attention should be paid to improving the water use efficiency of vegetation and optimizing the ecosystem structure. In the future, the study can be combined with high-resolution ground observation to further identify the recovery thresholds of different ecosystems, which will provide more accurate scientific support for the recovery and management of karst regions and similar fragile ecosystems.

## 5. Conclusions

Based on the extreme drought event in 2022, this study systematically assessed vegetation recovery time and its driving mechanisms across different lithologic ecosystems in the karst region of Southwest China. The results show that vegetation in the dolomite areas of the karst region suffered the greatest productivity loss and exhibited the most significantly prolonged recovery time, indicating high sensitivity to drought. Vegetation recovery in limestone areas was relatively faster but still limited by water availability and use efficiency. Multivariate analyses further revealed that water-related factors play a more important role in recovery within the karst region, with water use efficiency being a key controlling factor. In contrast, vegetation recovery time in the non karst clastic rock areas was shorter, with recovery primarily influenced by temperature changes rather than water limitation. Additionally, thicker soil and regolith layers helped facilitate the recovery process. In summary, the heterogeneity of vegetation recovery in karst ecosystems mainly stems from differences in water acquisition and utilization efficiency, while non-karst clastic rock areas exhibit temperaturedominated recovery mechanisms. Future integration of high-resolution ground observations and process-based models will help identify key recovery thresholds in different lithologic ecosystems. This will provide scientific support for managing and restoring karst and other fragile ecosystems.
